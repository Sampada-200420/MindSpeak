FOR A KINDER COMMUNITY

Learn to
Support Others

Be there. Listen. Understand.

Supporting someone doesn't mean having all the answers. Learn simple skills that can help you listen, communicate with care, understand emotions, respect boundaries, and know when extra support is needed.

“Sometimes the most supportive thing you can do is simply be there and truly listen.”
SUPPORT SKILLS

Small skills can make a big difference.

You don't need to be a counsellor or expert to offer meaningful support. Explore each skill and learn what you can do in everyday conversations.

01
SUPPORT SKILL 01

Active Listening

Be present and truly hear.

What does active listening mean?

Active listening means giving someone your full attention instead of immediately trying to solve their problem. It helps the other person feel heard, respected, and taken seriously.

You can try:

  • Put distractions aside.
  • Let them finish before responding.
  • Ask gentle follow-up questions.
  • Reflect back what you heard.
TRY THIS

Instead of giving advice immediately...

Instead of:

“You should just stop worrying about it.”

Try:

“That sounds difficult. Do you want to tell me more about what happened?”

02
SUPPORT SKILL 02

Supportive Communication

Have respectful and caring conversations.

How can you communicate supportively?

Supportive communication is about choosing words that show care without judging, blaming, or forcing someone to talk before they are ready.

You can try:

  • Use calm and respectful language.
  • Ask what kind of support they want.
  • Avoid judging their experience.
  • Remind them that they are not alone.
TRY THIS

Helpful words can sound simple.

Avoid:

“Other people have it worse.”

Try:

“I can see that this is really affecting you. How can I support you right now?”

03
SUPPORT SKILL 03

Understanding Emotions

Learn to recognise and validate feelings.

You don't have to fix every feeling.

People sometimes need their emotions to be acknowledged before they are ready to think about solutions. Validation means recognising that a person's feelings are real and worth listening to.

You can try:

  • Notice changes in their mood.
  • Name what you think they may be feeling gently.
  • Avoid saying that their feelings are silly.
  • Give them space to express themselves.
TRY THIS

Validation doesn't mean you agree with everything.

Avoid:

“There is nothing to be upset about.”

Try:

“It makes sense that you're feeling upset after what happened.”

04
SUPPORT SKILL 04

Setting Boundaries

Know your limits while caring for others.

Supporting someone doesn't mean doing everything.

Healthy boundaries help both people understand what you can and cannot provide. You can care deeply about someone while still protecting your own time, energy, and wellbeing.

You can try:

  • Be honest about what you can offer.
  • Don't promise more than you can manage.
  • Take breaks when you need them.
  • Encourage professional support when appropriate.
TRY THIS

A boundary can still be caring.

Try:

“I care about you and I want to listen, but I need some time to rest. Can we talk again tomorrow?”

05
SUPPORT SKILL 05

Safety & Referral

Know when extra help is needed.

You don't have to handle serious situations alone.

Sometimes a person may need support beyond what a friend, peer, or community member can provide. Knowing when to involve a trusted adult, professional, emergency service, or another appropriate source of help is an important support skill.

Look for situations where:

  • Someone may be in immediate danger.
  • Someone talks about harming themselves or another person.
  • The situation is beyond what you can safely manage.
  • Ongoing difficulties are seriously affecting daily life.

Remember

Your role is to support, not to become someone's therapist or solve everything yourself.

When a situation feels unsafe or beyond your ability to help, reaching out for appropriate professional or emergency support is part of caring for the person.

A SIMPLE REMINDER

Listen first. Care genuinely. Know your limits.

You don't need perfect words to make someone feel less alone.

READY TO SUPPORT SOMEONE?

A small conversation can mean a lot.

Put these skills into practice by reaching out, listening, and being present for someone who may need support.
